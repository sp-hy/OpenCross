# Battle rules

The lifecycle and turn rules of a fight: how a battle is built, what a command costs, who acts next, how statuses and the field ring change, when the fight ends and what it leaves behind. This document is the behaviour of `battle::Battle` and of the host code around it (field hand-off, session write-back, results screen). The formulas themselves (damage, hit chance, heal, status roll, stamina, colour multiplier values) are in `formats/battle_data.md` section 10, growth in its section 3, drop rows in its section 7 and the element modes in its section 5.2; the class API and the PAC VM are in `architecture.md` section 5; the script ops are in `formats/battlescript.md`. They are referenced here, not repeated. Unit numbers: 0 to 2 are the party slots, 3 to 8 the enemy slots. Colour indices: 0 green, 1 white, 2 black, 3 red, 4 yellow, 5 blue, 6 none (in the field ring 6 is the all-colour slot and -1 an empty one).

## 1. Battle start

### 1.1 From the field to `Battle::start`

* `make_field_battle_args` resolves the scene with `battle::encounter_scene`: an id of 0x200 or more (field op C9) is the scene `id - 0x200` and draws nothing; otherwise (op A3, a random encounter) one `rnd(0, 2549)` picks the variant from the 8 bands of `battle_data.md` section 6 (variant 0 half the time, each next one half as often, variant 7 10 in 2550) and the scene is `symtbl[id * 8 + variant]`. The roll uses a generator of its own, seeded from the battle seed (`OC_BATTLE_SEED`, set per fight by `[Debug] Seed`), so a seeded run repeats its enemy groups.
* Battle id and scene: only two tests read the field's battle id: the Relief Charm below (0x3DD) and the boss intro (an id above 0x1FF, `StartOptions::battle_id`). Every later test of the fight (scene 0x17F countdowns, 0x1D7 / 0x1D8 / 0x1DD music, results screen and New Game+ HP, mode 49's 0x1D8, the event-text table) compares the scene number.
* **Monster names:** a unit's name is the language's row 0x19 of the system text, like the Dopple list; the English table is the fallback when a row is missing.
* **The fight before Kid is named (scene 0x186, room mis_b02e):** her name on the HUD, the target cursor, the battle message window and the results reads a fixed per-language word whatever her typed name (`data::unnamed_kid_label`). Script text and every other scene keep her name.
* **The Lynx duel (scene 0x1DD, room ryu_b29i):** the party's leader fights alone: PC slots 1 and 2 stay empty, and the rest of the party only decides the enemies' bodies. The scene's three dummy enemies are drawn as characters (`Unit::body`), with their stats unchanged: unit 3 Serge, unit 4 Kid, each with the weapon of that party member or the default 0x22 / 0x29, and unit 5 the party member in script variable 0x1C8 if there is one. The original reads the party and weapons from a list scene 0x1B1, the room's previous fight, wrote sorted by id; no party change lies between the two fights, so `Battle::duel_roster_` keeps the party of the duel itself. On its duel entrance (parameter 1) the room moves Kid to party slot 2, or without her puts the lower character id in slot 1, then sets 0x1C8 to slot 1, so unit 5 is never Kid unless she is the only companion left in that slot. Each of them also takes the character as its kind byte, which the scene's script reads, and the gender word of the character record, which replaces the monster's before the gender is rolled and scales the HP. Unit 5 also takes the member's colour, and Pip stays in his current form: his kind comes from his record. A unit 5 whose character is not in the party stays the monster it is. With a relief member set, that member stands in the search list where Serge stood (the list comes from the fight before the duel, which the Relief Charm applies to), so unit 3 takes the default weapon and the member's own body takes the member's weapon. `BattleOptions::duel_third` carries the variable. The room makes the leader model 51 first. The dummies read as the character they became (`BattleOptions::duel_names`, the name rule of `menus.md` section 14: the Serge dummy reads Lynx while record 0 wears 0x31, and a Pip form past id 0x2B reads nothing); party member 0 reads Lynx in this scene.
* The party is the field party (`world.party_chars()`). Relief Charm: unless the battle id is 0x3DD, when the save's relief member is set and not already in the party, every party slot holding character 0 (Serge) takes the relief member instead.
* `battle_setup` adds: the New Game+ flag, the growth state (star from the save, progress from head 0x598 of the save, counter from the save) and the bench (every character record outside the party, recruited or not, that holds stats: the star-up grows them all; every record of a save the original writes holds stats).
* `Battle::start` fails (returns false, the host reports the fight as fled) for a scene id outside the table, a party of 0 or more than 3, or a scene that yields no enemy.

### 1.2 Party

* Party slot i (the field party order) takes unit i. Only the placement order follows the character id (the lowest id is placed when the load ends, the others with the party intro); the seat, the HUD panel and the unit slot stay the party slot.
* Per member (`init_pc`): stats from the save record; HP `min(saved HP, max HP)`; countdown 8 and AP 8; stamina 70; statuses `lo = saved persistent bits (mask 0x39) | permanent lo`, permanent lo = gear permanent statuses | 0x400400, `hi` = gear permanent statuses; status skip masks = armour mask OR defence-accessory masks (`Equip::resist_*`); gender from record word +0x14; stamina rate = record rate + gear stamina modifier, floor 0.
* Gear HP% (gear flag 0x01, once per accessory, in accessory order): the battle-start HP (the saved HP, never above max) becomes `HP x (pct + 100) / 100`, plus 1 when that changed nothing, cap 999; max HP stays, so the HP may start above it (the fight's end clamps the record's HP to max HP). PS1 and RDE capture: Pip at 85 / 85 wearing Earring of Hope (15%) starts at 97 / 85. HP 0 starts KO'd and is not raised (no fight leaves a record at 0 HP: the end sets it to 1).
* Gear flags are the OR of `flags & 0xF` over weapon, armour and accessories: 0x01 HP%, 0x02 gold x125/100, 0x04 deals x125/100, 0x08 takes x75/100. Accessory flags 0x10 start gauge (`power`), 0x20 offence (adds weapon power, accuracy and MAG), 0x40 defence (adds armour term, MDEF term, evade, status and guard masks), 0x80 stamina rate (`mag`) and status duration (`power`, signed: `Equip::dur_mod`, added to the wearer's duration stat when a timer starts). When no gear table is loaded, or neither the weapon nor the armour id resolves, the flat defaults of `Equip` apply (weapon 16, accuracy 60, variance 20, armour 4, armour variance 20, MDEF gear 4) and the accessories add nothing; their ids are still kept (`Equip::acc`), so the Dopplegang accessory 0xFC and the special-status accessories 0xF9 to 0xFB keep working.
* Pip: a kind byte 0x2B to 0x30 rewrites the cells holding element 0x11F / 0x121 / 0x123 to the form's first tech and 0x120 / 0x122 / 0x124 to its second (section 9), and sets the colour his 18 techs take to his innate colour. Any member whose grid holds element 0x7E (Chrono Cross) turns the colour-history row on for the HUD (`colour_row_shown`).
* Starting gauge: `-1 + gear start-gauge`, clamped to `[-1, gauge_max]`. `gauge_max` is the deepest grid row (cell / 10) that holds an unlocked cell with an element; -1 when there is none. Usable cells: unlocked, element id non-zero and inside the element table. A cell is unlocked exactly when the save's unlock mask of its row has the cell's bit (`party_member_from_state`), the same test the menu's Element screen uses, so the battle and the menu always show the same open cells.
* The relief member is only a different character in the slot; the rules see no difference.

* The permanent lo bits 0x400 and 0x400000 are the target info window's: 0x400 draws the HP / sex / colour block, 0x400000 the HP digits ("????" without it). Every unit gets 0x400 at spawn; a party unit (also a Dopplegang form and a monster-party pick) gets both, an enemy gets 0x400000 only from its record or a landed Infoscope (element 66). They are no timed status, no cure removes them, a knocked-out PC keeps them (6.5), and the menu's equip commit keeps both in record +0xA8 when it rewrites the gear statuses.

### 1.3 Enemies

Scene record (28 bytes, `battle_data.md` section 6): groups 0 to 2 in order; a group is skipped when its enemy id is 0xFF, its count is 0 or the id is outside `monster.bin`; each copy takes the next free enemy unit (up to 6). A group with count above 1 numbers its copies 1, 2, 3 ... (name suffix A, B, C ...); a group of one has no suffix. The scene's boss bit (byte 0x1A bit 7) makes it a star-up fight; the low 7 bits are the stage background; byte 0x1B seeds the all-target cast mask (section 3.3).

Per enemy (`init_enemy`, `load_monster_record`), in slot order:

| Source | Becomes |
|---|---|
| record +0xA8 / +0xAC | permanent status words `perm_lo` (OR 0x400 at spawn) / `perm_hi`; `hi` also carries the monster type bits 0x00200000 to 0x10000000 that weapon slayer masks and the element exploit test read |
| record +0x04 / +0x08 | weakness masks (the status chance of those bits is doubled) |
| record +0x0C / +0x10 | status skip masks, OR the masks of the record gear (armour and defence accessories) |
| ATK DEF HIT EVD MAG MDEF, medium and heavy hit bytes, innate colour byte, duration, stamina rate, grid | the same fields of the unit; usable cells = every cell with a valid element id |
| record gear | `Equip` (weapon, armour, accessories) as for a PC; flat weapon power 22 when no gear table is loaded |
| gold, drop row, drop base, model | `gold`, `drop_row`, `drop_base`, `kind` |

* Max HP: `min(record, 32767)`, then the New Game+ and gender scaling of `battle_data.md` section 3 (a record gender of 0 costs one RNG draw, `rnd(1, 2)`), final cap 32767. HP starts full.
* Action period: `ap = record AP; ap += rnd(0, ap >> 2)`. First countdown: `rnd(lo, ap)` with `lo = 1` for scene ids above 0x17F, else 0 (after the AP bonus, so the draw uses the larger value).
* RNG order per enemy: gender (if rolled), AP bonus, countdown.
* Enemies start with stamina 70 and gauge -1. Their `gauge_max` is the deepest grid row holding an element (`& 0x7FFF` non-zero) in a cell without the lock bit 0x4000, -1 when there is none; every stock monster cell carries the lock bit, so an enemy gauge stays at -1 (landed hits are clamped to it).
* Field ring: the three `btbgdat` colours of the stage's row: the battlefield operand when it names a row of the table, else the scene's background (the same choice the stage loader makes); a colour value of 6 or more leaves its slot empty (-1). Slot 0 is the oldest.

* Home facing: every unit slot, party and enemy, starts with `home_facing` = the scene record's facing byte of the slot (bytes 0x11..0x19) << 3, so the home yaw is `home_facing << 6` (4096 = 360 deg). Only battle-script op 85 changes it (`battlescript.md`); a form change keeps it. The view turns every living unit to it in the step after a party element command (`battle_stage.md` section 6).

### 1.4 Before the first menu

After the units exist `brain_.begin_battle` runs the scripts' init slot, then `settle()` (section 2.6) runs. Time does not advance while a PC can open a menu: only an enemy whose seeded countdown is 0 acts, one turn each in round-robin order from the first enemy slot, each followed by its reactions. Then the loop stops at the first point where a PC can open a menu.

## 2. The turn system

### 2.1 Clocks

* Each enemy has a countdown (0xFFFF = "is taking its turn now", never decremented). A PC has stamina (0 to 70, can be negative after a cast) and a countdown that matters only for confusion and Pip's form change (section 2.6). Both kinds of countdown run with the actions: every party action lowers the countdown of every unit in the turn order (present and not KO'd: a frozen unit counts, section 6.3), enemies and all three PCs (the actor included), by its steps (2.3), and every enemy action lowers the three PCs' countdowns by 3 (2.6); time advance (2.5) lowers them all.
* "Steps" are the unit of time: a weapon hit of strength s (0 weak, 1 medium, 2 heavy) is `s + 1` steps; a cast, a defend and an enemy cast are 1 step.
* Status upkeep (poison, regen, status timers, 2.4) is party time: it steps only after PC-led actions. The "attacked" scripts (slot 4, section 7.2) likewise start only after a PC-led action whose targets are all enemies.

### 2.2 Commands (`apply`)

`apply` runs `validate` (section 3) and does nothing when it fails. Then the acting PC's guard ends (a command comes from the PC's own menu, whose opening ends it; see the guard note). Per kind:

| Command | Cost and effect | After each unit of the command |
|---|---|---|
| Attack, 1 to 4 chained strengths (`kMaxChain` = 4) | each hit costs stamina (`battle_data.md` section 10; weak 10 / 12, medium 20 / 25, heavy 30 / 37 when fatigued; nothing while the actor has hi status 0x20000); the whole chain must fit the stamina at the time of the command, strengths must be 0 to 2; the cost is paid per hit, also on a miss | other PCs recover (2.3), enemies and PCs count down `s + 1`, `post_action(s + 1, s + 1)`, `check_end`; the chain stops when the battle ends or the actor is down |
| Element (a grid cell) | stamina `- 70` (nothing for a caster with hi status 0x20000); gauge `- (row + 1)`, floor -1; the cell is marked used (section 4). A double / triple tech (section 4.1): every caster pays 70 (unless it has hi status 0x20000) and gives up its cell; caster entry k's gauge becomes party slot k's gauge `- (its own cell row + 1)`, clamped to `[-1, slot k's gauge_max]` | other PCs recover 1 step (not the casters, not the target if it is a PC), enemies and PCs count down 1, `post_action(7, 1)` |
| Defend | stamina `+ 10`, cap 70; guard on | other PCs recover 1 step, enemies and PCs count down 1, `post_action(1, 1)`; then, when no PC could take the menu (`can_open_menu`, a guarding PC counted as unable), one `time_advance` (2.5) before the menu passes on |
| Run | the fight ends at once as Fled (2.7, 7): no roll, no cost, no check of the scene | none |

Notes:

* Automatic target. An Attack uses the given target only when it is a living, targetable enemy; otherwise (target -1, a party unit, a dead or off-stage enemy) it takes the living, targetable enemy with the lowest current HP (first on ties), and re-picks the same way inside a chain when the target dies. An Element is re-targeted only when its target is -1 or an empty slot: a self-only element picks the caster, a party-only element the living PC with the lowest HP fraction (compared by cross-multiplication, the caster first), any other the enemy as for an Attack.
* Chain counter: the PC's hit chance bonus (`battle_data.md` section 10) survives from one Attack command to the next only against the same target; a miss, a retarget or any other command resets it. Another unit's action (an enemy turn, a counter-attack, a confused or scripted PC turn) resets it too, except while the attacking PC's turn is still open (after its Attack it can still act): an action that interrupts the open turn ends the chain only when it aims at that PC or the PC's stamina is 0, so a PC whose turn an enemy hit on another PC interrupted goes on with its chain on the same target (`end_chain_after`). The remembered PC and target themselves survive every enemy action; an Attack with another PC or target starts a fresh chain.
* Guard: from a Defend until the PC's top command menu takes over (`open_menu`, called by the host whenever the top menu starts for a PC: the menu passing to it, an L1 / R1 switch landing on it, the menu reopening on it after enemy turns) or its KO. The menu does not take over the tick it reaches the PC: its window opens first (section 3.4), about 20 ticks during which the PC keeps the guard and the block pose. Other PCs' commands and time advances leave it on. It halves weapon damage taken (`battle_data.md` section 10; element damage is not halved), so a hit that lands after the PC's menu has taken over, even when the PC has not acted yet, is not halved. While it is set the unit's flag 0x200 is on and the idle script (anmseq op 0x49 reads the flag) holds the block pose; the script restarts when the flag changes, so the pose drops when the menu takes over.
* After the command: reactions (slot-4 and kill scripts, section 7), then `settle()` when the fight is still on.

### 2.3 Stamina recovery of the others (`step_after_pc_action`)

For every PC in the turn order (frozen ones included) except the actor, the other casters of a double / triple tech and (for a cast) a PC target: stamina rises by the recovery step of `battle_data.md` section 10 (modifier 0), capped at 70. The number of steps is the hit's steps for a weapon hit and 1 for a cast or defend. The same call lowers the countdown of every enemy in the turn order that is not 0xFFFF, and then of every PC in it (the actor included), by that number of steps, floor 0. A PC's scripted action (a confused turn, Pip's form element) ends the same way: it pays the stamina of a weak hit for a weapon entry whatever the entry's strength (10, 12 when fatigued, floored at 0) and 70 for an element (nothing with hi status 0x20000), and runs this call with 1 step, excluding only the actor. On the PS1 a confused Serge's queued heavy (strength 2) and weak entries both cost 10 stamina; with three PCs (Serge confused, the others free, countdowns 20 / 25 / 43 / 44) the weak entry on the third PC lowered every other countdown by 1 (19 / 24 / 42 / 43), as a menu weak hit does.

### 2.4 Upkeep per action (`post_action`)

Run once per hit of a chain, once per cast or defend, and once per enemy turn, scripted counter-attack plan or death cast (a plan: once for the whole plan, with the values of its last executed entry). `actions` is counted every time. The upkeep below runs only when the actor is a PC slot: its own command, its confused turn or its scripted turn (Pip's form element); enemy turns, enemy plans, counter-attacks and death casts leave poison, regen and every timer alone. For every unit in the turn order (present and not KO'd, a frozen one included), in unit order:

1. Poison (`lo` 0x1): HP `- poisonStep`. At 0 or below a PC is knocked out (`poison_kill`): HP 0, KO, stamina 0, guard cleared; of its inflicted statuses it keeps the `lo` bits 1 to 15 (poison and the `lo` bits 16 to 31 go, with their timers) and the whole `hi` word, so its stat modifiers stay. An enemy stays at 1.
2. Regen (`hi` 0x80000), if still alive: HP `+ poisonStep`, cap max HP.
3. Every timed status timer `- timerSteps`; at 0 or below the bit is cleared (and, for hi bits 6 to 11, the matching percent modifier returns to 100) and the timer removed.

`poisonStep` / `timerSteps`: weapon hit `s + 1` / `s + 1`; cast 7 / 1; defend 1 / 1; a scripted PC weapon entry 1 / 1 whatever its strength (a weak hit); a scripted PC element entry (a confused PC's script cast, Pip's form element) 7 / 1 like the menu's element command; a wait or a confused weapon attack without a script 1 / 1. Measured on the PS1 (scene 6, every unit poisoned, Serge confused so his slot-2 script queues): a heavy weapon entry took 1 HP of poison from every unit, an element entry 7, and a weapon entry stepped every status timer by 1 too (a timed hi status at 30 on every unit: 30 -> 29). The weapon steps are those of the party's command record, which the menu clears back to a weak weapon attack once a command has been carried out, so a scripted weapon entry always meets the weak record; with the record forced to a heavy hit the same entry steps poison and timers by 3 and costs 30 stamina. A forced queue entry written by `ps1_cast.py force` is no game path: the PC's command record stays at the menu's weak attack, so it is upkept as a weak hit (1) even for an element. A status gets its timer when it lands (section 6) and a repeated landing does not refresh it.

### 2.5 Time advance (`time_advance`)

Runs while nobody can act (2.6): once when the scheduler starts with no PC able to act (the menu finds nobody to pass to) and once in every scheduler pass after that, even when a unit is already waiting at countdown 0; and once after a Defend that leaves no PC free to take the menu (2.2). Because the step is the smallest countdown that is not 0, the first two steps of a stretch in which no PC can act usually bring every countdown to 0 at once (Serge alone, confused at his open menu: Serge 8, enemies 17 and 1 become 7 / 16 / 0, then 0 / 0 / 0, stamina refilled to 70), and from then on each enemy's new countdown is wiped by the next pass, so the enemies act one after another in scan order.

1. `step` = the smallest positive countdown among non-hidden enemies in the turn order that are not 0xFFFF; 255 when there is none.
2. Every unit in the turn order: countdown `- step`, floor 0 (not for 0xFFFF).
3. Every PC in the turn order: `stamina = min(70, stamina + step x rate)` (no flu factor).

The cheat state is applied first (section 10).

### 2.6 The scheduler (`settle`)

Loop (at most 20000 iterations; exceeding it ends the fight as Stalled):

1. `check_end` (section 7).
2. If no PC can act (`can_act`), time advances (2.5).
3. The nine units are scanned round-robin from the one after the last that acted; the first unit in the turn order (frozen units included) at countdown 0 takes its turn. A PC at 0 that is neither confused, frozen nor owed a form change does not: its countdown becomes its AP (8) and the scan goes on. A PC's turn is its script slot 2: Pip casts the pending form element (other PCs pass), a confused PC runs the plan its script queues, a frozen one whatever its script queues. A turn that queues nothing (a frozen PC, a PC whose confusion is only permanent, the other PCs during a pending form change) is no action: its countdown is reset, but poison, regen and the status timers do not step, so a frozen PC passing its turns stays frozen until its timer runs out through the other PCs' actions. The PC script attacks only while confusion is in the inflicted status word (read through field 0xB2), so a PC whose confusion is only permanent queues nothing and its turn passes without an action. Every scripted PC turn ends with the script's A4, which sets the countdown to `AP + rnd(0, AP >> 2)` after its actions (Pip's form change sets 21 during the action; A4 then replaces it). Only a PC with no script at all falls back to a weak weapon attack on a random living unit other than itself, then `post_action(1, 1)`. Each action of such a turn ends as described in 2.3. Then the reactions run and the loop restarts.
4. An enemy at 0 takes its turn, frozen or not: countdown 0xFFFF, then either the scripted plan (the script's slot 2; several queued attacks are one turn) or `EnemyBrain::choose`. A frozen unit's first executed action ends its freeze (section 6.3) and then runs as usual. Within a plan every element entry is its own action, presented as its own cast (its charge, pack and target ring), and the field ring takes its colour before the next entry starts, so a turn that queues Uplift, Fireball and Bushwhacker shows yellow, red and green one after another; consecutive weapon entries stay one action. Every action an enemy carries out (a turn, a counter-attack, a death or idle cast) lowers the three PCs' countdowns by 3, floor 0; a turn that queues nothing lowers nothing. Counter-attack and idle plans follow the same rule. A turn sets `countdown = ap + rnd(0, ap >> 2)` and runs `post_action`. An enemy element is cast from the grid row `level - 1` (0 when it has no level). A weapon aimed at a dead target retargets to the first living PC; an absent or out-of-range target becomes unit 0. The fallback brain (`RandomBrain`) picks a random living PC, then with 40 percent a random usable mode-0 element of its grid, else a weapon strength 0 / 1 / 2 with 60 / 30 / 10 percent. Then reactions run and the loop restarts.
5. If nobody took a turn and a PC `can_act`, the loop returns: the host picks which PC. While a form change is pending nobody can act, so time runs on until a PC's countdown reaches 0 and step 3 casts the form element.
6. Otherwise repeat.

`can_open_menu`: a PC, alive, stamina 10 or more, none of the `lo` bits 0x20304080 (KO 0x80, asleep 0x4000, confused 0x100000, 0x200000, defeated / frozen 0x20000000). A guard does not close the menu. `can_act` also requires the fight to be on and no pending form change.

### 2.7 Flee

Only `Command::Run` ends a fight as Fled; `validate` refuses it only when the battle is over or the actor cannot act (section 3.2). A scripted code 2 (section 7) ends it the same way. A Run while pilfered loot is pending (`Rewards::stolen` not empty) drops it: the list is cleared and the Run event carries system message row 5 ('Dropped the stolen goods!'). The view then holds the escape 80 ticks longer and opens that window at (32, 144) 40 ticks into the hold; the battle loop ends with the hold, so the window closes then.

## 3. Targeting and validation

### 3.1 Target codes

The element record byte +4 (`target_spec`):

| Bit | Meaning |
|---|---|
| 0x40 | the party (units 0 to 2) can be aimed at |
| 0x80 | the enemies (units 3 to 8) can be aimed at |
| neither 0x40 nor 0x80 | enemies only |
| 0x10 | the cast covers the whole range at once (3.3) |
| 0x04 | self only: the cursor cannot leave the caster |
| 0x01 | only knocked-out units qualify (Revive) |
| 0x02 | a both-sides ring opens on the party (the Cure line) |

Items and elements read the same byte. The target cursor (`target_cursor_order`) opens on the first eligible unit of an order given by the byte: self only, the caster; both sides, from the first enemy, or from unit 0 with bit 0x02, through all nine; the party (0x40, every consumable), from unit 0, not the caster; the enemies, from the caster's last confirmed enemy (the first enemy at battle start), wrapping within the enemies. A confirmed party target leaves that memory alone. Eligible means on stage, not knocked out and HP not 0 (a frozen unit is eligible: Attack and every element aim at it and land), or knocked out (or at 0 HP) for bit 0x01. An element with nobody on its ring (Revive with no one knocked out) is refused with sound 0x18 and the list stays open.

**Moving the cursor.** Only Left and Right move a single-target cursor; Up and Down do nothing. Which unit a press reaches depends on the camera option (Triangle in the target selection flips it):

* NORMAL: the nearest eligible unit strictly to the right (left) of the current one by view-space x under the current camera (the unit's position across the view, before the perspective divide). With none on that side the cursor stays: there is no wrap. On a tie the later unit wins.
* DYNAMIC: the eligible units ordered by the angle `atan2(z, x)` of their home positions on the battlefield floor, ascending (equal angles keep unit order). Left steps to the next unit and wraps from the last to the first; Right steps to the previous one and wraps from the first to the last.

Captured on the PS1 (scene 7, three enemies: unit 3 at home (-1280, 1792), 4 at (-1280, -2304), 5 at (-2304, -256); on screen 4, 5, 3 from left to right): NORMAL from 3, Right x3 stays on 3, Left 3 -> 5 -> 4 and then stays on 4, Up / Down stay; DYNAMIC Left 3 -> 5 -> 4 -> 3 -> 5, Right 5 -> 3 -> 4 -> 5 -> 3. RDE runs the same steps. OpenCross: `bhud::target_step` (`src/game/battle/hud_rules.h`), `BattleView::target_step`.

### 3.2 `validate`

Refused, with the reason string, when: the battle is over; the actor is not a party slot or `can_act` is false; an Attack chain is empty, longer than 4, has a strength outside 0 to 2 or does not fit the stamina; an Element cell is not usable (section 4.3); the target number is 9 or more; the target is an enemy that `untargetable` reports (hidden / off-stage units, script ops 34, 35 and 5B) and the command is an Attack or Element. For an Element with an explicit target, additionally: the target slot is empty; the target's side is not allowed by the target bits; a self-only element aimed at another unit; a KO-only element aimed at a unit that is not KO'd, or any other element aimed at a unit that is not alive.

### 3.3 All-target casts

`cast_element` (record byte +4 bit 0x10) hits every qualifying unit of the target's side, or all nine units when both side bits are set (colour fields, Chrono Cross, the seal-all elements). A unit qualifies when it is present, not KO'd and HP not 0, frozen or not (KO'd for a KO-only element or mode 4), and, for an enemy slot, bit `unit` of `cast_mask` is set; the party side is never masked. `cast_mask` starts as `~(scene byte 0x1B << 3) & 0x1FF`; script ops 34, 35 and 5B (argument 0) set a unit's bit. A cast reads the mask once, when it starts, so a unit a kill script reveals during the cast is not hit by it. The cast stops when the fight ends. If nobody qualified, the cast is made once on the chosen target. An enemy's scripted element (op 67) aimed at several units is likewise one cast: each unit of its target mask that stands (not KO'd, HP not 0, frozen included; KO'd for a KO-only element) takes it once. The ring takes the cast colour once, after the last target (section 5).

### 3.4 Guard and the command menu

See 2.2. The menu order (attacker, else actor + 1, actor + 2, the actor last) and the L1 / R1 switch walk the PCs by `can_open_menu` alone, so the menu can come to a guarding PC, which ends its guard. The guard counts as "cannot take the menu" only in Defend's own time-step check. It does not affect targeting.

**Menu entry.** Whenever the menu reaches a PC (the first menu of the battle, the end of a PC's turn, an L1 / R1 press, a PC that can act again after nobody could) the PC's command window opens before its top menu runs. The window rect (x 36, bottom y 204, PS1 pixels) grows by three sub-steps per draw pass, 3 px wide and 3 x 36 / 52 px high (8.8 fixed point), to 52 x 36: 18 draw passes. Until it is full only a white chamfered outline is drawn, from (x - 6, y - h - 6) to (x + w + 4, y + 5); the command box, its cursor and the command headline are not drawn. On the first frame after the window is full in which no action runs, the menu state steps on; on the next frame the top menu runs for the first time: the PC's guard ends and its idle restarts (2.2), the box is drawn, and from that frame on it takes cursor moves and confirms; L1 / R1 count one frame later. A button pressed before is dropped, not kept. The draw passes run once per video frame, so on RDE and in OpenCross (one game frame per video frame) the top menu takes over 20 ticks after the tick the menu reached the PC; the PS1, at two or three video frames per game frame, takes 7 to 11 game frames (20 to 23 video frames).

* When the menu passes: right after the PC's own action (Defend at once, an attack turn once the PC is home, an element once its cast and the menu's closing steps after it are over, `battle_stage.md` section 6), before the enemy actions that follow; the window grows during them and the top menu takes over on the second frame after the last of them, when the window is already full.
* L1 / R1 restart the menu entry even when nobody else can act (the menu stays on the PC, without the switch sound), and restart the PC's idle at the press, so a guarding PC's block pose plays again until its top menu takes over.
* Leaving the HIT% menu (Cross) is not an entry: the window stays open, and the top menu runs again on the first frame after the PC is home.
* OpenCross: `BattleView::begin_menu_entry`, `tick_menu_entry`, `build_entry_outline`; routes `battle_defend`, `battle_camera` and `battle_menu_entry`.
* Row colours of the command box (white 0x808080, yellow 0x8080, dim 0x404040). Top menu: Attack is dim at stamina exactly 0; Element is dim when the gauge is below 0, the stamina is exactly 0 or no cell of the first `(gauge + 1) x 10` is usable; Defend and Run Away stay white. HIT% menu: each strength row by its cost against the stamina (`battle_data.md` section 10; the fatigued thresholds 12 / 25 / 37), and while the PC has hi status 0x20000 (no stamina cost, 6.5) the three rows lose their red part, so white shows as 0x808000, yellow as 0x8000 and dim as 0x404000; the Element row there follows the top menu's Element rule. `BattleView` draws the HIT% figures with `Battle::hit_chance_shown_for`, not with the rolled chance.

**Confirm facing.** Confirming any top-menu command (Attack, Element, Defend, Run) turns the PC at once to the unit an enemy target cursor would start on (its remembered target, by the ring's start order); Attack's target ring then turns it with every cursor move. Defend keeps that yaw into the next menus: the end of the defending PC's action does not turn it home. Run: every living PC, the chooser included, then turns to yaw 0xC00 and runs (`battle_stage.md` section 6.2), so the chooser turns from its confirm yaw while the others turn from theirs. OpenCross: `BattleView::target_start`; route `battle_run_turn`.

## 4. Element grid and gauge

* The grid is 8 rows x 10 columns, cell = `row x 10 + column`, 80 cells. A cell word's low 14 bits are the element id; the top two bits are kept when a cell is emptied (`& 0xC000`).
* Gauge: starts as in 1.2. A landed weapon hit (the roll succeeded, whatever the damage code) by any unit adds `s + 1` (weak 1, medium 2, heavy 3), clamped to `[-1, gauge_max]`. A cast subtracts `row + 1`, floor -1 (not with the free-elements cheat).
* An element is usable (`element_usable`) when all hold: the cell is not colour-sealed; it is not a forbidden summon (below); the PC can open a menu and has stamina above 0; the cell is usable (unlocked, valid id) and not yet used this battle; `gauge >= row`.
* Seals: the PC's `lo` bit `0x800 << (4 x colour)` seals every cell whose element has that single colour (green 0x800, white 0x8000, black 0x80000, red 0x800000, yellow 0x8000000, blue 0x80000000); rainbow and colourless elements are never sealed. Sealed cells stay on the menu and refuse the confirm.
* Seals on any caster: an enemy's cells carry seal flags too, judged like a PC's (4.1) each time the enemy is the target of an action, and the enemy element pick (script ops 55 / 8F) skips a flagged cell. Whatever the caster, an element that can be sealed (id 0x12D at most and record byte 1 bit 0: the plain elements and their consumables, not the summons or the techs) cast by a unit whose `lo` seals the element's colour is cast as the forced miss: its mode becomes 50 (no damage, no status, a silent miss, no action-end message), and instead of its own pack it plays the seal pack of that colour, effect id 190 + c (c = 1 green .. 6 blue: `trap/green.prd` .. `trap/blue.prd`, eids 191..196). The rest of the cast is unchanged: the charge of the colour, the ring and the colour history take the colour, the cost is paid. The test is made once, when the cast starts. A scripted cast (op 67) by a sealed enemy so fizzles.
* Summons: a PC cell whose element (the double / triple tech the cell shows, else its own) has record byte 0 bit 0x40 is greyed and refuses the confirm (`summon_blocked`) unless the ring's summon colour equals the record's colour (byte +5 `& 0xFC`, as a ring slot, section 5) and at least one star is left (`GrowthState::counter`, the save's stars current). The ring's summon colour (`ring_summon_colour`) is judged whenever the ring changes: the colour every non-empty slot shares; none when two non-empty slots differ; an all-empty ring keeps the colour it had (none at battle start). Confirming a PC summon spends one star (not with the free-elements cheat); the battle starts from the save's stars current and the host writes the stars left back whatever the outcome (8.3). Enemy summons are not gated and spend nothing. While the drawn ring's non-empty slots share one colour (an all-empty ring included) the HUD shows the stars current / total under the ring caption (`BattleView::build_star_count`): a star mark at PS1 (62, 28) and "/" at (78, 27), both style 0x10005; digits style 5 at y 27 (Japanese 28): the current count's units at x 73 with its tens at 69, the total from x 84 (tens at 84 and units at 88, or units alone at 84); colour 0x8A8A8A, the same text in both art modes.
* Used cells stay used for the rest of the battle. Recharge: mode 12 (row 2 or higher) clears the used bit of the target's cells 0 to `10 x row - 1`; mode 3 (and mode 14 on a party slot) clears each used cell of the target with chance `variance` percent.
* Slot term: the per-mille factor of `battle_data.md` section 10 for the cell's row and the element's level; it scales damage and healing.
* Double / triple techs (`combo_at`, `cell_element`): see 4.1.
* Consumable stacks (element record flag byte bit 0x10): the stack size is the save's count (`PartyMember::stack` marks the counted cells). Each cast decrements it; the cell is marked used only when the last copy goes; with more than one copy the cell stays usable. A stack at 0 copies keeps its cell: every time the element menu opens (`open_element_menu`) such a cell is marked used, so it stays in the list greyed with its count "x0" and the confirm refuses it, whether it ran out in this fight or came in empty from the save (a recharge cannot bring it back). The count is written back to the party record. The free-elements cheat bypasses the stack, the used bit and the gauge.
* Element traps (mode 5): the caster's side stores the element id of record word +0xE (a different id replaces it). Before any cast of an element with a colour (record byte +5 `& 0xFC` non-zero, the raw record byte; the low two bits do not count) the other side's stored id is compared, for party and enemy casts alike. A colourless element is never caught, even when the trap holds its id: it casts normally (its own pack and mode, an empty slot pushed into the ring, section 5) and the trap stays set. On a match the cast fizzles (a miss event with no number and no effect; the ring still takes the cast's colour, section 5), the trap is cleared, and the cast still costs its stamina, gauge and used bit. Instead of its own pack the caught cast plays the trap pack of the element's colour (`battle_effects.md`, Caught casts) through the normal cast presentation (the colour's charge, then the pack; a caught summon plays the trap-summon presentation, not its own); its element mode never runs, so no number, miss text or action-end system message shows. `Battle::catch_in_trap` makes the check and the `caught` event. A fizzled PC cast also destroys the cell (`& 0xC000` in the battle grid and the party record, `Rewards::lost_cells`, written to the save by the host whatever the outcome). A fizzled enemy cast adds the element id to `Rewards::trapped`, which the host adds to the element stock whatever the outcome. `trap_hud()` reports the HUD block.

### 4.1 Double and triple techs

A tech cell of the PC whose menu is open turns into a double or triple tech while its partners can join; the menu then shows that tech (name, colour, description, target ring, the minimap plate) in the cell's place, and the confirm casts it. The substitution is worked out from the current state each time; only the seal flags and the menu tables described below are stored.

* Partner test, for party slot P and partner tech T: the first open cell of P whose word is exactly T (no lock or flag bits); P qualifies when that cell is unused and its seal flag is clear (the flag below, not the live status word), `gauge >= its row`, stamina is 10 or more and none of the `lo` bits 0x203000C0 is set (KO 0x80, 0x40, confused 0x100000, 0x200000, defeated / frozen 0x20000000). Asleep does not keep a partner out.
* The other two party slots are tried in slot order (the menu PC 0: slots 1, 2; PC 1: 0, 2; PC 2: 0, 1). Double (record +0x22 == 0, +0x20 != 0): the first of them that qualifies for +0x20 makes it the +0x26 tech. Triple (+0x22 != 0): each of the two slots must qualify for +0x22 or +0x24 (when both qualify the +0x24 cell is the one given up); the result is +0x28.
* The cell's own state is not tested beyond the usual element rules (section 4): a used tech still shows the double tech, greyed. The menu PC's own cells are sealed by the colour of the tech they show (the double tech's colour).
* The cast: the menu PC is the actor (its stats for every formula without a participant list); the casters are the record's character bytes +0x14..+0x16 matched against the party (`ActionEvent::group`, entry k with clip variant k, so X-Strike lists Serge first whoever chose it); every caster's cell is marked used and every caster pays 70 stamina (it may go negative). Each caster entry k then takes party slot k's gauge minus its own cell's `row + 1`, clamped to `[-1, slot k's gauge_max]`, entries in order; a caster that does not sit in slot k therefore takes another PC's gauge (Serge and Glenn's X-Strike with Kid in slot 1: Glenn's gauge becomes Kid's `- 3`).

* Seal flags and the menu build. Each PC cell carries a seal flag, and only the flag decides whether a partner joins. It is judged by the element the cell shows (`seal_flag`, `combo_word`): the double / triple tech where the last menu build put one into that cell, else the cell's own element; rainbow and empty cells keep their flag, and a cell is sealed when its colour's bit is in the PC's `lo`. The flag is judged again (a) for every PC at battle start, (b) for each PC that is the target of an action (any cast or weapon hit, once the action has run), (c) for the menu PC's own cells, all cleared first, when its element menu is built. It is not judged when a seal wears off or when only the partner's status changes, so a partner keeps a stale flag until one of the three happens.
* The element menu build (`open_element_menu`, run when the Element row of a PC's command menu or the HIT% menu is confirmed, and by an Element command first): every PC's menu table starts from its own cells; each of the menu PC's cells that offers a double / triple tech takes its result word, and so does the cell each partner gives up. The partners' tables keep those words (and so their flags are judged by the result's colour) until the next build. Consequences: a seal that lands on a partner after a menu build that showed the double tech is judged by the double tech's colour, so a seal of the partner's own colour leaves the flag clear and the partner still joins; a seal of the result's colour sets it. A partner's own menu build recomputes its flags, clearing a flag whose seal has run out.
* Auto battle (F5 / L3 + R3) only attacks. For the PC whose command menu is open it opens the Attack target cursor on that PC's remembered enemy target (the target it last confirmed, by hand or by auto, kept per PC and starting at the first enemy; when that enemy is gone, the next living enemy after it in unit order, wrapping within the enemies) and the HIT% menu picks by Accuracy Selection = Auto, whatever the option says. The Auto pick (`Battle::auto_hit_level`) runs each time the HIT% menu opens: the strongest strength k = 2, 1, 0 whose figure is above 79 and whose stamina need is met (30 / 20 / 10, fatigued 35 / 22 / 10). When the menu opens for a PC / target pair other than the last confirmed Attack or Element command (the last PC, and that PC's last target), the figure is the raw HIT stat of the strength minus the target's EVD x EVD% / 100 (no weapon accuracy, Hit% or chain); otherwise, and after each confirmed hit, it is the HIT% box figure (`battle_data.md` section 10). PS1, room-53 battle, Serge on a Komodo Pup with EVD 0: raw HIT 120 / box 99, 75, 65 opens on Fierce; raw 127, 50, 20 / box 99, 99, 99 opens on Weak; after the first hit both move by the box figures. It never Defends, Runs, rotates to another PC, or repeats an Element or Item; the menu sounds are muted while it drives, and results pages stay manual. The test driver (`BattleAuto`) uses the same rule unless `OC_BATTLE_AUTO_HEURISTICS` is set (see `docs/tools.md`).
* Confused PCs act by script or with a weak weapon attack, and the game's auto-battle only chooses attacks; neither ever picks a grid cell, so no combo substitution is involved. `auto_command` (tools and tests) is an OpenCross driver, not game behaviour: it casts through `apply`, which builds the menu first.

## 5. Field ring and colour

* The ring has three slots, oldest first. `ring_push` drops slot 0, shifts, and puts the new colour in slot 2.
* Colour multiplier (`colour_multiplier`): inputs are the attacker's innate colour, the attack colour (a weapon attacks in the colour of the attacker's colour status, hi bits 0..5 set by the Turn elements, else in its innate colour; every element command, weapon techs included, in the record's colour), the ring, and the target's colour. The values are in `battle_data.md` section 10. A ring slot of 6 (all colours, the Chrono Cross) and an empty slot count as no colour. Mode 6's field state (`battle_data.md` section 5.2) scales the multiplier of every element cast; no timer is modelled, so the state lasts for the rest of the battle once set (it is only set while it is 0).
* Timing: during a cast the ring is not changed, so every target of the cast sees the ring as it stood before. When the cast has finished (once, after the last target of an all-target cast) the ring takes the new state:
  * a repaint replaces all three slots: mode 6 with a colour in record word +0xE `& 0xFC` (all three become it), mode 37 (slots from record bytes +0xA, +0xC, +0xE, each byte `<< 2` read as a colour mask), mode 47 (a random colour that some PC lacks);
  * otherwise the cast's colour is pushed: the effective element colour (below) `& 0xFC` as a single bit gives 0 to 5, all six bits (0xFC) gives 6, anything else (a colourless element) gives -1 (an empty slot, which still shifts the oldest colour out). A missed or no-damage cast pushes too.
* Sources: element casts of PCs and enemies, including scripted casts and casts caught in an element trap (section 4). Weapon attacks never push.
* No-shift cases: an element whose record byte 0 has bit 0x80 (death casts, form changes), an element trap (mode 5).
* Effective element colour (`element_colour`): the record byte +5, except Pip's 18 techs, which take Pip's innate colour mask (`& 0xFC`) with the record's low 2 bits, and Vary Innate (element 336), which keeps its last caster's innate mask from its first cast on.
* Innate absorb: the colour mask of the innate byte is bits 2 to 7 (`& 0xFC`); innate bit 1 makes the unit absorb elements of its own colour. The armour's colour byte works the same way: bits 2 to 7 are a colour mask and bit 1 set means elements of that colour are absorbed. A target absorbs an element when it has the absorb hi status or `(absorbMask & element colour byte) != 0`; it is immune with the immune hi status (`battle_data.md` section 10 for the result codes).
* Armour colour boost (factor in `battle_data.md` section 10): with bit 1 of the armour colour byte set, elements of one colour are raised, chosen by the byte's colour bit:

| Armour colour bit | Element colour raised |
|---|---|
| 0x04 green | black |
| 0x08 white | white |
| 0x10 black | green |
| 0x20 red, 0x40 yellow | none |
| 0x80 blue | green |

* Colour shields (`hi` bits 0 to 5, one per colour; factors in `battle_data.md` section 10): when several bits are set the highest colour index is the shield; it compares against the element's colour, opposite or same.
* The colour history (`seq_colour`) is separate from the ring: 0 to 5 for a coloured element, 0 for an element without a colour mask, 6 for the all-colour element, -1 for none (record byte 0 bit 0x80). `ActionEvent` carries the first seven entries for the HUD.

## 6. Status application

### 6.1 `apply_status_bits(target, lo, hi, chance, colourBit)`

1. A target with the immune hi status (`kHiImmune`) takes nothing.
2. If `hi` has any of bits 29 to 31: when one of the target's accessories is 0xF9 to 0xFB ("special"), the element's colour (`colourBit >> 2`) joins `hi` as shield bits, and a permanent bit of 29 to 31 that the new mask repeats first makes the target's permanent bits 0 to 5 and 29 to 31 leave both its permanent and live words; without such an accessory, `hi` is cut to bits 6 to 28.
3. If `hi` (with the colour joined in step 2) has any shield bit (0 to 5), the target's inflicted shields are replaced: its `hi` bits 0 to 5 return to the permanent ones.
4. For set 0 (`lo`) then set 1 (`hi`), bit 0 to 31: skipped when the bit is not in the mask or the target is not alive; skipped when the target's skip mask has the bit; otherwise the bit lands without a roll if "special" and it is a hi bit below 6 or above 28, else when the status roll (`battle_data.md` section 10; the weakness mask is the doubling bit) lands. A roll that passes counts as landed even when the bit was already set; a new bit is set and, when it is timed, its timer starts. Each bit rolls separately. "Already set" reads the target's `hi` word as it was before step 3 (bits 0 to 5 and 29 to 31 cleared from that copy in the special case), so a shield colour the target already had is cleared by step 3 and not set again, and its timer is left running.

Element statuses use the record's `lo` word, `hi` word `& 0xE007FFFF`, chance byte +0xA and the element colour (`apply_status`).

### 6.2 Skip masks

PC: the OR of the armour's and the defence accessories' (flag 0x40) masks. Enemy: record +0x0C / +0x10 OR the masks of the gear on its record. Weakness masks exist for enemies only. A mask bit means the status never lands.

### 6.3 Timed statuses

A bit is timed when it is in `kTimedLo` (0xBBBFFB00) or `kTimedHi` (0x0003FFFF). The countdown walks every `lo` bit but only `hi` bits 0 to 17, so the special `hi` bits 29 to 31 (6.1, step 2) never run out: once inflicted they last the battle. Its timer starts at the status duration of `battle_data.md` section 10 (status index = bit, plus 32 for hi), from the unit's duration byte with no modifier. Timers count down in `post_action` (2.4). A landed weapon hit wakes a sleeping target and ends its confusion (clears `lo` 0x4000 and 0x100000 with their timers; a permanent bit stays, so a unit whose confusion is permanent stays confused); element mode 32 does the same, and a weapon tech (mode 29) does it after each of its hits unless the record's `lo` status mask holds the bit.

Frozen (`lo` 0x20000000) newly landing through either status setter (the roll or `force_status_bits`; the blue colour curse holds it) adds 3 to the target's countdown word (16-bit, so a unit at 0xFFFF wraps to 2) and marks the unit. The marked unit's next executed action (a weapon hit or a cast; a turn that queues nothing is no action) first clears `lo` 0x60000000 with their timers and the mark, then runs: a frozen enemy whose countdown reaches 0 thaws at its own turn and acts. Until then a frozen unit is out of `alive()` (the party's wipe test; the enemies' victory test counts it, section 7.1) but keeps its place in the turn order: its countdown runs with every action and time advance, and the timer thaws it as any timed status. It stays a target (`Unit::standing`): the party's target ring and all-target casts, every script target pick, count and element target (`battlescript.md` 3.3) and the attacked scripts take it, a hit on it lands (room-53 battle, both enemies frozen: Serge's weak hit takes one from 13 to 11 HP, the Frozen bit and timer stay), and statuses roll on it. Example: Ice Blast at 100 % on an enemy at countdown 1 (scene 6, Serge alone): 1 -> 4 at the landing, 3 / 1 / 0 after Serge's weak / medium / heavy hits (timer 13 -> 12 -> 10 -> 7), its turn at 0 clears Frozen before its weapon hit on Serge; cast by Serge, the cast's own step makes it 1 -> 3.

### 6.4 `force_status_bits`

Used by the colour curse (mode 36): `lo` bits only, no roll and no weakness doubling; skipped when the target is not alive, already has the bit or has it in its skip mask; timed bits get their timer.

### 6.5 HP application order

1. Each element mode orders its parts: mode 0 rolls the status first, a landed status cancels the damage when record word +0xE bit 15 is set, and with bit 14 a target with hi 0x2000000 is knocked out outright; modes 32 and 33 damage first, then roll the status; mode 15 rolls the status first and deals damage only when nothing landed.
2. `damage_unit` (an enemy's element action has already reduced the value, `battle_data.md` section 10): a KO'd target is untouched (HP stays 0, no healing). Code 3 heals up to max HP. Code 2 or a negative value does nothing. Otherwise HP `- value`; a target with `lo` 0x40000 also loses 10 stamina; HP at or below 0 becomes 1 for an invincible unit and for a self-inflicted hit, else `kill`.
3. `kill`: HP 0, KO bit (0x80), guard cleared, and for an enemy its kill script starts (section 7). A PC also loses every inflicted status except `lo` 0x400480 and `hi` 0x1FE00000 (bits 21 to 28), together with their timers; the permanent (gear / innate) statuses stay. The six percent modifiers keep their values (only a timer running out resets one to 100), so a PC revived after a buff or debuff still has it in its stats. Stamina and the countdown are untouched until a revive (`battle_data.md` 5.2, mode 4: stamina 70, countdown `AP + rnd(0, AP >> 2)`). So a revived PC comes back without poison, sleep, seals or a colour status, and `finish` carries no poison / sprain / flu of a PC that was knocked out by a hit into the save.
4. A unit is alive when it is present and its `lo` has neither KO (0x80) nor defeated / frozen (0x20000000). A status that sets one of those bits removes the unit from play without touching its HP; a frozen unit still keeps its countdown and its turns (6.3).
5. Drain (hi status 0x40000): when a unit with it deals damage through a weapon hit, a weapon tech or an element damage pass (modes 0, 14 on an enemy slot, 15, 32, 33, 37, 39, 46; not mode 35), it is healed by the value dealt (a weapon tech: the total after its second gear step, healing the last hitter). The heal is owed once per action, for the last target damaged, and applied after the targets (an enemy element action's value reduced as in `battle_data.md` section 10). The heal shows no number: the drainer's HP rises silently on its status panel. No shipped record grants the status.
6. No stamina cost (hi status 0x20000): the unit's weapon hits and elements cost no stamina (2.2, 2.3), and its HIT% menu rows are drawn without their red part (3.4). Only Vigora (element 54, mode 1, 100 %) grants it.

Captured on the PS1 (all twelve statuses in both columns): every triangle colour, the y rows and the left columns match; the right column is drawn at 268 there.

### 6.7 The menu cursor

The red diamond left of the selected row of the command box (the top menu and the HIT% box) and of the Dopplegang creature list is one of two frames of the battle menu sprite sheet, both one part at offset (0, 0) of the draw point, so they share the top-left corner: frame 1 the 9 x 8 diamond, frame 2 the 7 x 8 diamond turned edge-on (`battle_11.png`: the left 9 and the right 7 of its 16 columns). The frame is `((battle tick >> 4) & 1) + 1`: each shows for 16 ticks, a full turn takes 32, and the phase follows the battle's tick counter, not the moment the menu opened (`BattleView::add_menu_cursor`). The command box draws it at (box x + 2, box y - 42 + 10 x row) in the original's coordinates; nothing else about it changes (no colour, brightness or position animation). The original also leaves it out on frames when the disc drive is busy; the engine has no disc and always draws it. The element list has no diamond (its selected plate pulses and the grid column blinks instead).

### 6.8 The stamina bar

Each party panel draws its stamina bar as three rectangles at (panel x + 8, 164 + 35): a black 52 x 4 frame, a black 50 x 2 track one pixel in, and the fill over the track. The fill is `|stamina| * 100 / 140` pixels wide with `|stamina|` (tenths) capped at 70, so a full bar at 7.0 and at -7.0 or below; it is never narrower than 1 pixel, so a bar at 0.0 (or between -0.1 and 0.1) still lights the track's first column. Its colour comes from a 16-entry table: for positive stamina entry `stamina / 10` (red below 1.0, yellow 1.0 to 3.9, cyan from 4.0), for negative stamina entry `8 + |stamina| / 10`, which is red throughout. So a PC below zero shows a red bar growing with its debt beside the "Out.-" label. RDE draws the bar through the same rectangles, so HD and Classic agree. Captured on the PS1 (fill widths at stamina 7.0, 3.5, 0.9, 0.1, 0, -0.1, -0.2, -0.9, -1.0, -2.5, -4.0, -6.9, -7.0, -7.5, -15.0: 50, 25, 6, 1, 1, 1, 1, 6, 7, 17, 28, 49, 50, 50, 50). OpenCross: `bhud::stamina_fill`, `BattleView::build_party_panels`.

## 7. Battle end

### 7.1 `check_end`

Run before every scheduler step, after each hit of a chain, after each plan entry and after reactions. In this order:

1. The cheat state is applied (section 10). Nothing happens if the outcome is already set.
2. Script op 81 can switch the whole test off.
3. Forced code (script op 83, non-negative): 0 ends as Victory (rewards are paid), 1 as Wiped, 2 as Fled, any other as Scripted with that code.
4. Wipe: no PC alive (a frozen PC counts as down) ends as Wiped, at the next test: Serge alone frozen loses the fight at once. This test comes before the victory test, so a mutual KO is a defeat.
5. Victory: for each enemy slot that is present, the fight continues if the unit is in the turn order (not KO'd; a frozen enemy counts, so freezing every enemy wins nothing and the fight goes on until they are beaten) or still owes a scripted death (`death_pending`, 7.2), and (it owes a death, or it is not hidden). Hidden (off-stage) living enemies therefore do not block the win. When no enemy blocks, the fight ends as Victory.

`finish` sets the outcome once. The result code is the forced code, else the `Outcome` value: Victory 0, Wiped 1, Fled 2, Scripted 3 and up (the script's code), Stalled 9. `Outcome::Stalled` is set only by the scheduler's 20000-turn guard.

### 7.2 Scripted deaths

A killed enemy runs its kill script (slot 3) once, when its HP reaches 0 by damage or by a script write. Only a resolved hit is a KO for the model (dead flag, death clips): HP written by a script leaves it up (`Unit::hp_written_zero`, `battle_effects.md`). Death casts it queues (op 67) are executed by the dying unit after the killing action, each op 67 as its own action (its targets share one: the Time Devourer's 341, then 526). A result the kill script forces (op 83) takes effect only once all its casts have run (the script sets it after its end-turn op A4). The unit blocks the victory test (`death_pending`) until the cast has run; the presentation additionally holds the unit's death while the script flag of op 5C is set (cleared by the script's A6). After an action led by a PC slot (its command, a confused or scripted PC turn) the target list, in ascending unit order, is checked: when its lowest unit is an enemy (no PC among the targets) each living target runs its "attacked" script (slot 4), one after the other in that order; the attacks it queues are executed like any action (counter-attacks), with death casts run before and after. Enemy-led actions (turns, plans, counter-attacks, death casts) start no slot 4, nor does a PC heal or buff on the party or a PC cast whose targets include a PC, so counters never chain. A death-cast action is an ordinary `post_action` / `check_end` action.

The self-destruct casts (element mode 40: the Bomber's Suicide Bomb 348, Potpourri's Toxic Burst 387) are such death casts: the hit pass rolls the element's status first (Toxic Burst poisons), then the target loses half its HP before the hit (an absorber is healed by it only when the damage roll was not zero; an element-immune target takes nothing). The caster's own entry takes the same half with the same code, which changes nothing on the dying, knocked-out caster (`formats/battle_data.md` section 5.2, mode 40).

The Radius tutorial (battle 386) has one more rule, RDE's and applied in every session (the PS1 has none): its first enemy slot (unit 3, Radius) never dies. Each hit's pending damage on it is cut to its HP - 1, so the number shown is the cut value and it keeps at least 1 HP, and a KO on it does not stay (no kill script, no death). The tutorial's own idle script rewrites that unit's HP every pass (1024; its 50 branch needs var 0x84 >= 2, and the only increment of var 0x84 sits in a subroutine nothing calls), so in play the cut is seldom reached; it only keeps the scripted tutorial from losing its teacher. RDE applies the rule when the battle was requested by a field battle op whose operand is 386 (Arni's `BATTLE_200 #386`), which is the only way scene 386 is reached in play. RDE capture (Serge alone, battlefield 22, a Volcano cast from grid row 8 by MAG 127, which kills a 1024-HP Radius when the rule is off): HP 1 and no KO bit on the frame the damage lands, 1024 again on the next frame, the tutorial goes on; the same cast in a battle 386 not requested through that op leaves HP 0 with the KO bit and Radius leaves the field. OpenCross gives HP 1 and no KO, and the idle script's next pass (the settle after the action) writes 1024. OpenCross: `Battle::undying`, `god_filter`, `kill`.

### 7.3 What `finish` records

The party records: `hp = min(unit HP, record max HP)`, raised to 1 if 0; persistent status = `lo & 0x39` (bit 0 poison, bits 3 and 4 the burn and sprain statuses, bit 5 flu). Rewards (section 8) are computed first, for Victory only.

### 7.4 Host mapping (`finish_field_battle`)

| Outcome | Code to the field |
|---|---|
| Victory | 0 |
| Wiped | 1 |
| Scripted | the script's code |
| Fled, Stalled | 2 |

* The battle counter that RDE keeps goes up by one (cap 0xFFFF) for code 0 or 3.
* `apply_battle_result` runs for every outcome (section 8.3), the Dopplegang mask goes back to the world, the results screen's HP is copied (8.5), then `world.end_battle(code)`.
* Losable fights: the encounter's flag 2 ("losable"). A wipe (code 1) without it loads the game-over room (map 9); the title is the fallback if that load fails. With flag 2 the field simply continues with result 1 (`architecture.md` sections 5.6 and 12.3).
* A battle that cannot start counts as fled (code 2).

## 8. Rewards and after-battle state

### 8.1 Gold and drops (`compute_rewards`, Victory only)

* Only enemies in the defeated mask pay: those that are not alive, plus those a script marked defeated (op 8C). A living, unmarked enemy (a forced win) pays nothing.
* Gold: the sum of the paying enemies' record gold; `x 125 / 100` (truncating) when the start option says so or any PC in the fight has gear flag 0x02 (Profiteer Purse). The host adds it to the save's gold, cap 9,999,999.
* Drops: no roll at all after script op 7C (the gold is still paid). Outside boss fights an enemy that died while a summon (element record byte 0 bit 0x40) was the current action gives that summon's shiny material with no roll: green Shiny Leaf, white Shiny Salt, black Shiny Soot, red Shiny Ember, yellow Shiny Sand, blue Shiny Dew. The action stays current until the next one starts; the first colour to claim an enemy keeps it. Every other paying enemy: `r = rnd(0, 99)`; `r < dropBase / 20` gives drop row slot 1 (rare), else `r < dropBase / 2` slot 0 (common), else nothing (`battle_data.md` section 7 for the rows; the row is the record's drop row `& 0x3FFF`, as for the pilfer). The word's bit 15 makes it an element (`id & 0x7FFF`, `Rewards::elements`, added to the stock with that id), otherwise a non-zero item id (`Rewards::items`). The roll is consumed even when the row is outside the table.
* The pilfer (mode 30) loot is separate: `Rewards::stolen`, added to the stock on a victory only (a flight or a defeat loses it).

### 8.2 Growth (Victory, star below 99 and inside the star table)

Row `{battles, pendingTenths, step}` of the star table (`battle_data.md` sections 1 and 3 for the table and the formulas); `cap = (short)(step x battles)`. The Lv 99 values below are the member's `lv99para.bin` record; for Pip in a form they are his form's (section 9.1).

* Normal fight: `progress = min(progress + step, cap)` (the instant-growth cheat sets it to `cap`). For every party member that is alive: for each of the 7 stats, `gain = minor_growth(snap, pending, stat, pendingTenths, progress, cap)` (the aim and rounding of `battle_data.md` section 3, not round-half-up; with the instant-growth cheat `full_minor_growth`, the whole distance `(snap + K) - stat` with the same aim `K`, floor 0), limited to the room below the Lv 99 value; the stat is raised and the gain recorded in `Rewards::gains`. Max HP is capped at 999 (the core raises the stat, not the current HP). Knocked-out members get nothing.
* Boss fight (scene boss bit): `star_up`. Every living party member and every bench member (the bench regardless of state) gains its whole pending delta, limited to the Lv 99 room. Then `progress = 0`, `star + 1`, `counter + 1`. Then everyone (the whole party including the knocked-out, and the bench, merged and rolled in character id order with the battle RNG) takes `snap = stat` and new pending deltas (`next_pending_hp`, `next_pending_stat`), and their battle-side grid unlocks are recomputed: the populated cells of rows 0 and 1 plus the `el_mask` unlocks up to the new star (only the rest of this fight reads them; the save's masks are written by the host, below). The original applies all this as the results screen closes, after the power-up pages. PS1, scene 398 won from the room-53 encounter with only Serge and Kid recruited, Serge in the party: Serge took his pending gains, and every other record (Kid and the 42 characters not yet recruited) took its old pending delta (max HP 81 + 12 = 93 for Kid, 85 + 14 = 99 for record 3) and a new snapshot equal to the new stats.

### 8.3 What the host writes (`apply_battle_result`)

For every outcome, per party member: the 7 stats (max HP clamped 1 to 999, the others 0 to 127), current HP `clamp(member HP, 1, max HP)`, persistent status `lo & 0x39`, the growth snapshot and pending words, Pip's form change (section 9), and the consumable stacks (the counted-down copies are stored; a stack that ran out keeps its element at 0 copies and shows greyed with "x0" in the next fight, section 4). Then every consumable stack cell of the member below 5 copies takes `min(5 - copies, stock)` copies from the element stock (a stock slot that reaches 0 is freed), whether or not the fight used it. Then the trap results (section 4): trapped elements and lost cells are written, and the stars current (the summons spent them, section 4; clamped 0 to 255). On a victory only, the stolen loot (8.1): stolen words go to the element stock (bit 15, `id & 0xFF`, like a trapped element) or the item stock. The growth progress goes back to head 0x598 of the save. Stamina is not carried; every battle starts at 70.

For Victory only: gold, dropped items and elements (an element drop uses `id & 0x7F`), and after a boss fight the star byte (cap 99) and the stars current (a star-up adds one to both), the grid unlocks, and the bench's new stats and growth words. The grid unlocks (new star below 99): in all 44 records, recruited or not, in the party or not, every `el_mask` cell of stars 0 to the new star (the record's growth column) that exists in the record's grid gets its bit in the unlock mask, whether the cell holds an element or not. A character who joins later therefore finds every earlier star's cells open, and the menu shows each new cell at once. Every save written by the original game carries these masks (`formats/save.md` section 6).

### 8.4 Results screen (`BattleResults`)

* It opens for Victory, Fled or Scripted outcomes unless the result code is 1, 3 or 4 or the scene is 0x1DD. A flight or a scripted code of 5 and up shows it without rewards; the scene 0x1D8 plays muted unless the code is 2.
* Pages in order: the star-up page (boss fights only), then one "PowerUp" page per party member that has any stat gain (a blank PowerUp page when nobody gained; the first member's gains are applied when the screen opens; showing a member adds its HP gain to its displayed HP, cap 999), the heal menu when offered, the "Healed" page listing the healers used (after a heal choice), "Spoils" (items and elements), then the exit.
* Exit: the last confirm plays the exit sound 0x41 only (no sound 8); the screen fades to black over 30 ticks while the panels slide out and stays black. The victory music plays on at full volume until the field returns, 43 ticks after the confirm: the field restores its song (which cuts the victory song, no fade); the field's fade-in from black starts 8 ticks later, the screen staying black until then (`BattleResults::kFieldReturn`, `kFieldFadeDelay`).
* Victory music (`BattleView::tick_finish`): the battle theme plays on after the win; 43 ticks after it the theme fades to silence over 16 ticks, and 20 ticks after the fade began the victory song (0x16, 0x17 after a boss) starts. Battle flag 0x10 and the scenes 0x1D7 / 0x1D8 / 0x1DD play no victory song.
* Heal menu rule: offered when at least one present, living member has HP below max HP and the healer list is not empty. Healers are the healing elements (menu-side byte +B bit 0x40, byte +A bit 0x40 clear = not a consumable) found in the open cells (unlocked, unused, id below 0x80) of rows 0 to the gauge of every living member, merged by id (the count is the number of cells), ordered by tier (menu-side byte +A & 3, tier 2 first, tier 3 excluded; equal tiers in discovery order, each member's cells scanned from the highest down), single-target before all-party (byte +A bit 0x80) when fewer than two members are hurt and after otherwise. Tier amounts: 80, 160, 320 (the fourth value, 16, is never used). The menu has three entries: heal, heal with stock (identical, the stock list is empty), do not heal (and cancel does the same). Healing applies each healer in order, up to its count of uses, until nobody is hurt: a single-target element heals the first hurt living member, an all-party element every hurt one, capped at max HP. No cell is marked used; only HP changes.
* Confirm debounce is 16 ticks, fade-in 60, settle 7.

### 8.5 `take_final_hp`

After the screen is done it returns, per party slot, the displayed HP `clamp(HP, 0, 999)` (HP after the fight, plus the growth's HP gain, plus the heals) or -1 for an empty slot; it returns false when no results screen ran since the last call. The host writes `clamp(HP, 1, max HP)` over the value `apply_battle_result` stored, so a knocked-out member ends at 1.

## 9. Pip's evolution and Dopplegang

### 9.1 Pip's form change

* The six colour points are script variables 0x04..0x0E, which start as the save's variables 0x3D4..0x3DE (`BattleOptions::saved_vars`, variables 0x3D0..0x3DE as script variables 0x00..0x0E) and go back to them after the fight (`Battle::saved_vars`, not after a monster-party fight). Every element action adds 1 to the point of its record colour while Pip (character 43) is in the party and 1 more when Pip casts it (`battlescript.md` section 3.1).
* Script op A7 reads Pip's kind byte and the six colour-point script variables (green, white, black, red, yellow, blue) and stores the form element 0x80 to 0x85 (or 0) in a script variable; a non-zero result also sets `evo_pending` (`battlescript.md` for the thresholds). Every PC entity's idle loop runs it on its own unit each tick while that unit's kind is 0x2B..0x2D.
* While `evo_pending` is non-zero no PC gets a command menu; every PC whose countdown reaches 0 takes its script slot 2 turn: Pip casts the pending element on himself, the others pass.
* Mode 45 of that element calls `Battle::evolve(unit, form)` with the record's power as the new kind (0x2B to 0x30 only):
  * the innate colour mask becomes the colour with the most points (the last of equal ones; all zero gives blue), keeping innate bits 0 and 1;
  * the six colour-point script variables are zeroed and `evo_pending` cleared;
  * the unit's kind byte, AP (8) and countdown (21) are set; the grid cells (row 2, 4, 6; column 7) take the form's techs from `kPipTechs`, keeping the cell's top two bits, the third cell only for a second form (kind above 0x2D), in the unit and in the party record; a unit cell that is empty stays empty (the party record cell is written all the same); every rewritten unit cell loses its used bit, so a tech Pip spent before the change can be cast again in the same fight; Pip's techs take the new colour;
  * the party record gets the kind, `evolved = true` and the growth column of the form (`kPipGrowthCol`: 3, 3, 3, 4, 5, 6).
* The event is silent (no number), result code 1, and carries the new kind for the presentation. An element with record byte 0 bit 0x80 leaves the ring unchanged (section 5).
* The host (`apply_battle_result`, any outcome): writes the kind into the record's model id (the growth column follows it) and the 2 or 3 tech cells (`& 0x1FF`).
* **Growth by form (RDE; the PS1 never grows Pip by form).** Pip in a form 0x2B..0x30 grows toward the `lv99para.bin` record of his form (records 0x2C..0x30 for the evolved forms; 0x2B is record 43) instead of record 43's: `PartyMember::lv99` is that record (`party_member_from_state`, and `Battle::evolve` the moment the form changes). It is the target of every growth clamp (the minor growth of a normal fight and the star-up gains) and of the star-up's next pending roll, for Pip in the party or on the bench. The first star-up after an evolution pays the pending deltas rolled before it, at the old form's rate, up to the new form's targets; every later one pays deltas rolled against the new form. RDE capture (Serge and Pip, Pip as Divine Beast 0x30 at 50 stars with record 43's Lv 99 stats 750 / 80 / 70 / 90 / 25 / 40 / 35 and pending 30 HP and 3 / 5 / 5 / 5 / 9 / 6, boss scene 398 won from room 53's encounter): 780 HP and ATK / DEF / HIT / EVD / MAG / MDEF 83 / 75 / 95 / 30 / 49 / 41, snapshots equal to the stats, next HP pending 2 (0 against record 43); the next boss win from there: 782 HP. The PS1 gives the same fights 0 gains and pending 0.
* **The 99-star form change (RDE; the PS1 changes nothing else).** When a fight ends with Pip's record in another form than at its start (both 0x2B..0x30) and the game has 99 stars, the last step of the fight, after the results screen has closed and its HP has been written (`game::settle_pip_form`), gives him the form's Lv 99 record (`newgame::pip_form_stats`: max HP, the HP snapshot and the six stats from `lv99para.bin`, the current HP moved by the change of max HP and clamped 0..max HP, the six stat snapshots equal to the new stats; the pending words stay) and ORs into his masks those of a full 99-star growth in that form (`newgame::pip_form_unlocks`: 0x40 in rows 0..2 plus every `el_mask` cell of the form's growth column, with no cell-existence test). Any outcome counts; the star count is the one after the fight. RDE capture (99 stars, Serge and Pip, Pip 0x2B at 500 HP, 7 / 7 / 85 / 20 / 8 / 7, 100 red points so his first turn casts the form element; room 53's encounter, won): the record takes kind 0x2C at the change, keeps its stats until the results screen closes, then holds 780 / 780 HP, 84 / 80 / 90 / 25 / 44 / 36, snapshots 84 / 90 / 80 / 25 / 44 / 36, pending 8 HP and 0 / 0 / 0 / 0 / 1 / 0 unchanged, masks f8 f8 f8 f0 e0 c0 0 0. While the results screen is still open the record has none of this, and a capture that stops confirming once the field is back sees the old stats.
* **On load.** A card slot whose `ext_save.dat` pair is not valid (a slot RDE never saved; `formats/save.md` section 9, load rule 3) runs the same stat sync on load when Pip is in an evolved form 0x2C..0x30 at 99 stars, without the masks. A slot RDE or OpenCross saved holds a valid pair and loads as saved.

### 9.2 Dopplegang

* The list's first row is the caster: its own name, typed or default, with no Lynx rule (`PartyMember::own_name`).
* The pool is 256 bits in 16 words (`BattleOptions::dopple_mask`, the save's 16 words, script variables 0x60 to 0x7F). Bit `n` of word `w` is pool slot `w x 16 + n`. `dopple_pool` lists the set slots ascending.
* Credit (`dopple_credit`), called by script op 65 on an enemy (the start of its kill script) with the last PC that commanded (script variable 0x50): it sets the enemy's pool slot (monster record byte +0x2B) when that PC is character 26 (Sprigg) or wears accessory 0xFC, and the enemy's record byte +0xA6 bit 0x80 is clear. It reports whether the bit was new.
* `transform(unit, form)` (the unit swap the pick menu requests): `form >= 0` makes the PC take monster record `form`: stats, innate colour, gear, status words (with 0x400400 OR-ed into the permanent `lo`), the monster's grid (its cells lose the lock bit, `& 0x3FFF`) and its name; the form then grows with the stars left (`growth.counter`, after the summons this fight spent): `extra = stars left - (record +0xA6 & 0x7F)`, and when that is positive ATK, DEF, MAG and MDEF each add `extra >> 4`, `>> 3` or `>> 2` by their 2-bit selector in record +0xA7 (0 = none), capped at 255 (`battle_data.md` section 3); HP stays, max HP becomes that of the last unit that changed form from a character body (her own, or Pip's after his form change), stamina stays, AP and countdown become 8, gauge -1, and `gauge_max` becomes the deepest row of that grid holding an element (-1 for none); inflicted statuses and timers are gone; the character's unit is backed up the first time. `form < 0` restores the backup, its stamina, AP, statuses and used cells included, with the current HP, countdown 8 and the max HP of the last unit that changed form from a character body, which the party record keeps. It fails (returns false) for an unknown form, a non-PC unit, or `form < 0` on a unit that has no form.
* Big Catch (mode 39) draws creatures from the same pool (`battle_data.md` section 5.2).
* The host persists only the mask: `finish_field_battle` writes `dopple_mask()` back to the world after every fight (not after a monster-party fight, 9.3), together with `saved_vars()` (9.1). A creature form is never written to the party record: a PC still in a form when the fight ends leaves it as its own unit (`Battle::exit_unit`, which the end of the fight and the results screen read): the unit backed up before the form, with the HP of now, while she was the last unit to change form from a character body and her creature form the last form change of the fight; once a Pip form change has come after hers (with or without a later creature form), the whole backed-up unit, its HP from before the form included. The original keeps one "restore at exit" flag and one slot: a creature form change sets the flag, the way back and every Pip form change clear it; the slot is the last unit that changed form from a character body (a creature form from the own form, Pip's form change). With the flag set the exit puts the backed-up unit into that slot with that slot's HP of now. With the flag clear the PS1 copies the creature unit into the results and the record keeps the creature's kind byte and stats; RDE adds a battle-exit step that puts the backed-up unit back whenever the kind byte differs from the record's, which is the behaviour followed. Captures on the PS1 and RDE (room 53's encounter, Sprigg at 300 HP turned into form 0x43, her form HP set to 123, the enemies finished): Serge + Sprigg: both leave her at 123 HP with her own stats and kind 0x1A (124 / 301 after the HP growth, 204 after the results heal); Sprigg + Pip + Serge with Pip's form change after hers: RDE 300 HP (301 / 301 after the growth), her own stats and kind, the PS1 kind 0x43 with the creature's stats in the record. The flag the original could hold back is written only by its debug program. Creature form, Pip's form change, then a creature form again until the end: the flag is set but the slot is Pip's, so the PS1 and RDE both copy Sprigg's backed-up unit into Pip's slot with Pip's HP, and Pip's party record becomes a copy of Sprigg's (kind 0x1A, her stats, gear and grid, max HP 300, 301 after the growth); RDE's exit step then gives Sprigg her whole unit back (300 / 300; the PS1 leaves her record the creature at 85 / 85). OpenCross gives Sprigg her whole unit and leaves Pip's record as it was (the overwrite of Pip's record is not modelled). The way back always restores the backed-up unit (every swap start sets the flag, so the way back finds it set and clears it): its stamina, AP, statuses and used cells, with the HP of now, countdown 8 and the max HP of the last unit that changed form from a character body, which the party record keeps (it grows from there at the results). Captures on the PS1 (room 53's encounter, Sprigg at 300 HP + Pip at 85 + Serge, Sprigg into form 0x43, her HP held at 123, the backed-up unit's stamina set to 30 and hers to 50, then she picks herself in the creature list): 123 / 300 and stamina 30; with Pip's form change before the way back 123 / 85 and stamina 30 (RDE the same), the unit block equal to the backup but for HP, max HP and the countdown, the results 123 / 85 and the record 135 / 135 after the growth. A creature form taken after Pip's form change gets his max HP too (PS1 and RDE 123 / 85).

### 9.3 Monster-party scenes (the Grand Slam)

* A scene whose record byte 0x0F has bit 0 set (`battle_data.md` section 6) is a monster-party fight when the host passes picks (`BattleOptions::monster_party`, the Grand Slam picker's three pool slots, `rules/menus.md`); without any pick (dev tools) the party fights as usual.
* Party slot i becomes monster record `pick[i]` (`init_party_monster`; a slot without a pick stays empty) under the slot's character id: the whole record (stats, innate colour, status words with 0x400400 OR-ed into the permanent `lo`, skip masks, grid, gear, name, record gender word). Max HP = the record's (NG+ doubles it outside scene 0x1DD, cap 30000), then max and current HP are capped at 999; AP and the first countdown 8, stamina 70; gauge `-1 + gear start-gauge` clamped to `[-1, gauge_max]`, `gauge_max` the deepest row holding an element in an unlocked cell.
* In such a fight every monster record's grid (the picks' and the enemies') loses the flag bits (0xC000) of the cells in rows 1 to 7 outside column 8, so their elements can be cast; row 0 and column 8 keep theirs.
* The end writes nothing back: no rewards (gold, drops, growth), no party HP or status, no results screen, no Dopplegang pool.

## 10. Cheats as the rules see them

`Battle::Cheats` is pushed by the host every update (`set_cheats`); `set_invincible(party, enemies)` is the separate debug switch. All are off by default.

| Flag | What it bypasses |
|---|---|
| `invincible` (debug party / enemies; for PCs also God mode and Battle boost) | `kill` leaves HP at least 1 (no KO, no kill script); damage never takes HP below 1 |
| `god_mode` | every damaging hit on a PC becomes value 0, code 1 (heals untouched); each time advance and each `check_end` revives KO'd PCs, refills HP, clears the negative `lo` statuses (mask 0xAA9EE939, frozen included, so a frozen PC is freed) and their timers |
| `boost` | everything God mode does, plus gauge to `gauge_max` and stamina 70 on every living PC |
| `max_stamina` | living PCs' stamina 70 at each time advance and `check_end` |
| `free_elements` | a cast does not mark the cell used, does not spend a consumable stack and does not spend gauge; stamina is still paid |
| `always_hit` | the hit chance of a PC weapon attack is 100 (also the chance figure the pilfer reads) |
| `always_loot` | a PC's pilfer skips the chance roll and takes the rare word when it is non-zero; kill drops take row slot 1 when non-zero, else slot 0 (the drop roll is still drawn) |
| `instant_growth` | a normal victory sets `progress` to `cap` and raises every stat straight to its minor-growth target (`full_minor_growth`), so one win gives all the growth the star's normal fights can give and later wins give nothing until the next star-up. Every grown member (and, on a boss's star-up, every member the star-up grew) is also raised to `typical_star_growth`: the mean (rounded) of 256 star-up histories (`star_growth`), each the stats the star-ups up to the current star give the record's starting stats, every pending delta rolled as the star-up rolls it for every record (from a generator seeded by the character id, not the battle's). Fights' minor growth is not part of it. In a normal fight the raise is measured from the snapshot (the star's start) and moves the snapshot too, before that fight's minor growth, so the star's minor growth still comes on top; after a star-up it is measured from the new stats. No stat is lowered, each stops at Lv 99 (max HP also at 999), and the raise is part of the fight's gain on the results page, so a character that missed earlier star-ups (for example one recruited with a record that never grew) catches up in one win |
| `shared_growth` | a normal victory runs the same per-stat growth for the knocked-out party members and every bench member (gains in `Rewards::gains` / `bench_gains`; the host writes back the stats of the bench members that are recruited, so a character not yet recruited keeps its record; a knocked-out member stays at 0 HP); a boss victory also gives the knocked-out members their pending deltas |
