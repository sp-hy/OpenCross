# Versus battles (multiplayer)

A versus battle is an OpenCross multiplayer feature, not an RDE one: two players' parties fight each other (`formats/multiplayer.md` section 8 covers the challenge that starts it). It reuses the battle rules of `battle.md` everywhere except where this document says otherwise. Normal battles are unchanged: every rule here applies only to a battle started as a versus battle.

## 1. Sides and units

- Side A is the player who moves first (`pvp_start` `first`), side B the other. Both games number the units the same way: side A's party in units 0 to 2, side B's in units 3 to 5; units 6 to 8 stay empty.
- Every unit is a player character with its own stats, weapon, element grid, stamina and techs, as `battle.md` section 1.2 builds a party member. Every rule that `battle.md` gives "the party" or "the enemies" applies to a unit's own side and the other side: targeting, auto-targets, weapon sweeps, all-target casts, the element gauge, double and triple techs, stamina recovery, poison / regen / status upkeep, traps (one trap slot per side), summon star counters and the Dopplegang pool (per side).
- Element and summon values are the party's on both sides: the halving and quartering of enemy-cast elements (`battle.md`) does not apply.
- "The party" and "the enemies" are read from the caster's side wherever an element names them: a double or triple tech takes its partners from the caster's side only, an element that hurts the enemies and heals the party (mode 14) hurts the other side and heals the caster's, the field colour that some party member lacks (mode 47) is looked for on the caster's side, and Pip's techs take the colour of the caster's own Pip. An element caught by the other side's trap is lost, whichever side cast it (no element stock).
- No battle script runs: neither side has an enemy script, so the colour history that only scripts keep is empty (the Chrono Cross element cannot be cast), Pip does not evolve, a confused character takes the built-in weak hit, and Steal finds nothing on a character.
- Each game shows its own player's party in the party positions and the opponent's party in mirrored positions on the enemy side, facing it. The opponent's HP and stamina are not shown.

## 2. Turns

- The sides alternate. A side's turn lasts until its player chooses End Turn (a new entry of the command menu), none of its characters can act (out of stamina or knocked out), or 60 seconds pass; the timeout ends the turn as End Turn does. Three timeouts in a row by the same side forfeit the battle.
- Side A starts. On its opening turn only, each of side A's characters starts at stamina 35 (3.5; `pvp_start` `opening_stamina`); side B's start at 70. After that, stamina only changes by the rules.
- Within a turn the player commands any of their characters, in any order, with the normal menu (attack chains, elements, defend). Run is not available as an escape: it surrenders the battle.
- The Dopplegang pick is a command of its own, given right after the cast that opened it; the pick may keep the caster's own form, which still closes it. A cast that spends its side's last stamina opens the pick all the same: the side that owes the pick acts next, before the other side's first command of its turn (the menus stay shut until it has come).
- Defend sets the guard and gives the defender its 10 stamina (cap 70) as in `battle.md` section 2.2, and ends that character's actions for the turn: it cannot be commanded again until its side's next turn starts. A character that is done counts as unable to act.
- Confused and frozen characters take their turns when their countdowns reach 0, as in `battle.md` section 2.6, whichever side holds the turn; the others' countdowns only restart at 0.
- Recovery: each action of one side counts, for every character of the other side, as an action by another member of its own party (`battle.md` section 2.3): weak, medium and heavy hits give 1, 2 and 3 recovery steps, an element or a defend 1, each step worth the character's recovery rate, Flu applied, capped at 70. During a side's own turn its characters recover nothing from that side's actions; a character of the side that does not hold the turn (a confused one's scheduled turn) recovers its own side as in `battle.md` section 2.3.
- A side whose characters can no longer act during its turn (all done, out of stamina or knocked out) passes the turn at once. Since the turn side regains no stamina from its own actions and every Defend ends a character's turn, a turn ends once its side's stamina is spent.
- The status that waives stamina costs in other battles (hi 0x20000) waives nothing in a versus battle: weapon hits and elements cost their stamina as usual, the HIT% menu's budget is the stamina alone, and the status otherwise lands and runs out as it does elsewhere. Every turn is therefore bounded.
- A side whose turn starts with no character able to act advances time as `battle.md` section 2.5 does, with the step taken from the other side's countdowns (every unit's countdown runs it, only this side's characters recover), for as long as one of its characters is held back by its stamina alone (alive, no status that keeps its menu shut, a recovery rate above 0); a side that time cannot help passes the turn when the other side can act or be helped, and when neither side can, time runs for every unit (the step from every countdown, every character recovering) so that the turns of confused or frozen characters come. A battle in which nobody can ever act again is a draw.
- Defend's time step for a party with nobody free to take the menu (`battle.md` section 2.2) does not run: only this section passes the turn.
- Three timeouts in a row means three turns of the same side ended by a timeout; a turn of that side ended any other way (End Turn, or passed because it could not act) starts the count again.
- Party swap, auto-battle, the cheats, fast and slow mode, the pause screen and the debug tools are off for the whole battle.

## 3. The start and the end

- Both games build the battle from the same inputs: the seed of `pvp_start`, both parties as exchanged when it starts (full HP, no statuses: a sandbox), and the battlefield, picked with a generator seeded from the battle seed among the battlefields of the ordinary scenes (no boss flag, at least one enemy group, background below 100; sorted, each once). The regular battle music plays.
- A side wins when every character of the other side is knocked out (or frozen, as the party test of `battle.md` section 7.1 reads it). When the last characters of both sides go down on the same action the battle is a draw. Run (surrender), three timeouts in a row, or a player who stays disconnected for 30 seconds lose; so does, on the other game, a player whose game sends nothing for 90 seconds while its side should act (`formats/multiplayer.md` section 8.4). A desync (the games' battle states differ after a command, a command arrives out of order, or a game's rules refuse the other game's command) ends the battle with no result.
- There is no turn limit.
- Nothing of the battle reaches the save: no experience, growth, items, element stock, star progress or game over. The results screen is replaced by a Victory / Defeat / Draw plate, and each player returns to the field exactly as they left it.

## 4. On screen

- The battle is drawn on the stage of the lowest ordinary scene (as section 3 counts them) with the picked background. Each player's own party stands on that scene's party spots; the other party stands on the same spots mirrored left to right and turned half a turn, so the parties face each other, and comes on with the enemies' fade-in. A knocked-out character of either side stays on its knocked-out pose, as a party member does (it can be revived).
- The command menu has five rows: Attack, Element, Defend, End Turn, Run Away. Run Away asks "Surrender?" (No first); Yes surrenders.
- A banner at the top shows "Your turn" with the seconds left, or "<name>'s turn". The 60 seconds count while the player can give commands, not while actions are being shown. While the other side holds the turn the player's menus stay shut and its actions play as enemy actions do. While a player is out of reach the banner reads "Waiting for <name>... N" (the other player's connection is down) or "Reconnecting... N" (this game's own), N the seconds left of the 30, in the game's language.
- The target window shows the other side's names, sex and innate colour, with "????" for its HP as for an enemy not yet scanned; the party panels show only the player's own party. Damage numbers show for both sides.
- The element-trap block shows the player's own trap and only whether the other side holds one; the star count shows the player's summon stars left of those brought into the battle.
- The outcome plate (Victory, Defeat, Draw, or No result when the battle ended without one: "Battle out of sync" and the other game giving the battle up read No result, the other player gone for good reads Victory, this player gone for good Defeat) follows the win's victory poses and music or the loss's fade, takes a confirm after 1 second and closes by itself after 5; then the field fades in as after any battle.
